# cheap-mem — proof of closure attempt

The claim under test was mine, from the previous round:

> "Nothing fundamental is open at this trust boundary."

**It is withdrawn.** Three fundamental failures were found by attacking it.
All three are fixed; the report below is what replaces the claim.

---

## A. Disproved assumptions

**"Status is enforced."** It was computed from the *result set*. A
supersession or dispute whose line did not match the query was invisible,
so a retired claim came back `active`. The attacker picks the wording:
write a poisoning claim matching the queries you want, in words the
victim's claim does not share, and the whole authorisation layer is
bypassed without breaking a rule. Status is a property of the LOG.

**"The rules bound what a writer can do."** They bound what a writer can do
*to other people's claims*. A writer who only writes their own — own scope,
own tier, no supersession, distinct bodies — could remove the answer from
the memory entirely. Measured: 20 000 plausible contradictory claims, and
the single user-tier truth was **not returned at all**.

**"Ranking ≠ truth is a documented limitation."** It is not merely a
limitation. When volume decides *candidacy*, the memory stops being able to
produce what it contains — and that is integrity, not ranking (§H).

---

## B. New fundamental findings

1. **Candidacy is where integrity is decided, not ranking.** No policy
   applied after the top-k can recover a claim that was never a candidate.
   Every quota, filter and tier rule ran correctly and none of them
   mattered.
2. **A per-author quota cannot bound a single-author corpus.** Half of an
   answer that is entirely the attacker's is still entirely the attacker's.
3. **The two ways to use authority in retrieval are both wrong alone.**
   Precedence buries a relevant agent finding under a stale user note; pure
   relevance lets volume erase a tier. Representation in *candidacy* plus
   relevance in *ordering* is the pair that works.

## D. New invariants

- **I18 status-from-log.** A retired claim is excluded regardless of
  whether the line that retired it matches the query.
- **I19 tier representation.** A claim of any tier that matches the query
  at all can reach the answer regardless of the volume of other tiers.
- **I20 no tier precedence.** Within the answer, relevance orders; a
  higher tier does not outrank a more relevant lower one.
- **I21 contested is flagged, never resolved.** Same topic, same scope,
  overlapping validity, different authors → flagged with no winner picked.

## E. Mutation coverage

**41 mutants, 41 caught** (was 16). Twenty are *semantic* — a boundary
flipped, a comparison inverted, a default reversed — rather than
mechanism-removal. That distinction produced every finding here: all three
failures had every mechanism present and running.

Semantic mutation exposed **five false-confidence tests of mine**, two of
them written in this same session for these very fixes:

- 120 identical claims that deduplication collapsed, so a resource cap was
  never exercised
- a `.gitattributes` fixture that did not contain the word `jsonl`, so a
  substring match would have passed
- a `narrow()` assertion that never attempted to widen
- a noise fixture that outnumbered the truth without out-scoring it
- a user claim that shared no query terms and so was never a candidate

The pattern is one sentence: **a fixture must prove it exercises the
mechanism, or the assertion tests the fixture instead of the code.**

## F. Property and fuzz coverage

Property tests over seeded random logs: replay determinism and idempotence,
order independence within a timestamp, tie-break stability, index
equivalence (exact layer), scan stability, purity of analysis.

`bench/fuzz.mjs`: 7 parsers plus the capability boundary, 1500 seeded
rounds each, looking specifically for garbage producing a *more permissive*
answer. Clean, and verified to have teeth by breaking `admits()`.

Reading the two that matter most:

- **Provenance is CONDITIONAL in the core, GUARANTEED at the gateway.**
  The gateway always carries author, tier, scope and status with every
  claim. Whether those fields are *true* is the core's problem, and the
  core cannot verify them — hence conditional. `source` is a claim ABOUT
  provenance, not provenance.
- **Integrity of the ANSWER is conditional even now.** Candidacy is fixed,
  contested is flagged, but a memory can still be filled with plausible
  falsehoods by a rule-abiding writer. The memory returns the answer *and*
  says the subject is contested. It cannot say which claim is true.

## H. Remaining fundamental risks

**One, and it is epistemic rather than a defect.**

**Semantic contradiction is undecidable without a world model.** The core
can decide: identical bodies, explicit supersession, overlapping validity,
same scope, same topic, different authors. It cannot decide that "runs in
Frankfurt" and "runs in Berlin" conflict, that "not in Frankfurt" negates
"in Frankfurt", or that "primary server" and "server" may be different
subjects. Negation, quantifiers, granularity, perspective and time-shifted
phrasing are all out of reach.

Against the five criteria the audit set:

- *Why is it not a foundation problem?* Because the memory does not
  silently pick a side. It returns every contested claim, flags the
  subject, and leaves judgement to a reader with a world model.
- *Which invariant protects against it?* I21 — contested is flagged, never
  resolved. Resolution happens only where it is mechanical.
- *Which attack was tested?* `bench/byzantine.mjs`, and the German
  contradiction cases in the audit brief, none of which the core can
  decide.
- *Which mutation would disprove the claim?* "contested claims are dropped
  instead of flagged" and "one author saying two things counts as
  contested" — both caught.
- *Why is it outside the boundary?* Deciding it requires either a world
  model (a second model call per pair, which breaks "model in exactly one
  place") or a human. Both are outside a deterministic local core.

**Everything else is conditional, not fundamental**, and each condition is
named in §G and checked by `mem doctor --strict`.

## I. Remaining non-fundamental risks

- Linear search; WAND designed, trigger p50 > 25 ms (~2 years out).
- No semantic recall: a paraphrase with no shared vocabulary is not found.
- Near-duplicate flooding: exact bodies collapse, one changed word does
  not. Every similarity threshold is uncalibratable.
- The watermark is per machine and deletable.

returns the score to where it was wrongly held, and it cannot rise while
identity is a JSON field.

## The claim, restated

> At this trust boundary, one fundamental limit remains: **semantic
> contradiction is undecidable without a world model.** It is not a defect
> — the system refuses to resolve what it cannot judge, flags it, and
> returns everything. Every other known weakness is conditional, and every
> condition is named and checked.

That statement is weaker than the one it replaces, and it is the one the
evidence supports.

**What this round proves about the method, not the code:** the previous
claim was wrong, and it was wrong in a way that reading could not reveal.
It took a semantic mutation and a rule-abiding attacker to find it. A
verification round that cannot disprove its own conclusion has not verified
anything.
